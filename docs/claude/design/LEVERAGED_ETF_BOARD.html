How each rule is calculated
  • Data: IBKR daily bars, two years. A bar for today is dropped until 4:15 pm New York time, so a half-finished day never counts as a close.
  • 1. Trend: passes when the ETF's last close is above its 200-day simple average. The underlying's position is shown next to it, because 2x and 3x funds drift lower over time and can look worse than their sector.
  • 2. Pullback: passes when the trend passes and RSI(2) is below 10 (Connors' oversold dip). RSI uses Wilder's smoothing, the same formula as STA.
  • 3. Support & resistance: swing lows and highs over the last 60 days (a bar lower or higher than the 3 bars on each side). Passes when the nearest support is no more than 1 ATR below the close. Simpler than STA's full S&R engine.
  • 4. Consolidation: passes when the 20-day high-to-low range is at most 3.5 × ATR, meaning price has gone quiet. This threshold is a judgment call, not a tested number.
  • 5. Simple indicators: RSI(14), RSI(2), 8 and 21-day EMAs, 50 and 200-day SMAs, ATR(14) and relative volume. Shown as numbers, not counted.
  • Background (not counted): relative strength is the underlying's return minus SPY's over 3 and 12 months. Relative volume is today's volume over the prior 50-day average. Regime is SPY vs its 200-day average plus the VIX level (above 30 is high, matching STA).
  • Splits: each load asks IBKR for corporate actions and also flags any one-day move of 50% or more as a possible split to check.
