Swing Trade Analyzer · How the Stock Picker Thinks

Two ways this tool decides "is this stock worth a look?"

Every ticker you analyze runs through one of two paths. Both ask similar questions — one is a strict yes/no checklist, the other blends four broader judgments. Neither picks a stock for you; both narrow the list down to what's worth your own judgment.

Passes / Strong — a real green light
Borderline / Decent — okay, not great
Fails / Weak — a real problem

Before either path: how do you even get a candidate list?

Scan Market tab

Neither path picks a stock out of thin air — you feed it one. The scanners are that upstream step: instead of guessing tickers, each preset asks "which stocks currently match this specific thesis" across the whole market (or a scope you pick — All US, S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow 30, TSX 60, All Canadian) and hands back a short, ranked shortlist to run Simple/Full Analysis on.

Run it any time and get back Confirmed / Retest / Watch / Warning / Building-Base / Not-Ready, most-actionable first.

❌ Don't have: it telling you first

Both are explicitly on-demand — the skill's own file says it outright: "no push notifications, no cron, no auto-trading." Nothing watches your list in the background and pings you when something changes. That would be new work, modeled on the paper-trading engine's own scheduled job, not a flip of a setting.

Path 1 · Simple Checklist

9 yes/no questions. All 9 must pass.

No partial credit — this path is deliberately unforgiving. It's built to say "not yet" far more often than "yes."

  1. Is it trending up?
    Price above its 50-day average, which is above its 200-day average
    1 / TREND
  2. Is it beating the market?
    Outperforming the S&P 500 over the last year
    2 / MOMENTUM
  3. Can you get in near a safe exit?
    Entry sits close to a support level — a tight stop-loss
    3 / SETUP
  4. Is the potential win worth the risk?
    Reward is at least 2× the risk (2:1 or better)
    4 / RISK:REWARD
  5. Is it near its yearly high?
    Sitting in the top 25% of its 52-week range
    5 / 52-WK RANGE
  6. Is it easy to buy and sell?
    Enough daily dollar volume for its size (small/mid/large-cap)
    6 / VOLUME
  7. Is the trend real, not just noise?
    ADX of 20 or higher — a confirmed trend, not chop
    7 / ADX
  8. Is the whole market healthy? ← this is "Regime"
    S&P 500 above its 200-day average — bull market intact
    8 / MARKET
  9. Is that long-term trend still climbing?
    200-day average itself rising over the last month
    9 / 200-SMA TREND
Regime is just one bulb out of nine here. A healthy market lights up #8 — it does nothing for the other eight. Every single one still has to pass on its own.
9 / 9 → TRADE anything less → PASS no in-between — this path is all-or-nothing
Path 2 · Full Analysis

4 broad judgments, blended by a rulebook.

Each category is rated Strong / Decent / Weak, then a set of priority rules — not a simple point total — turns those four ratings into BUY / HOLD / AVOID.

📈 Technical
How healthy the chart looks right now
Strong — great trend, RSI in the sweet spot
Decent — okay trend, nothing alarming
Weak — poor or broken trend
🏦 Fundamental
How healthy the business is
Strong — high returns, real growth, low debt
Decent — nothing outstanding, nothing bad
Weak — shrinking, indebted, or unprofitable
😊 Sentiment
How fearful or greedy investors feel — shown for context, doesn't change the verdict
Strong — healthy greed, momentum-friendly
Neutral — mild fear or mild greed
Weak — extreme fear or extreme greed
🌦️ Risk / Macro ← "Regime"
Is the overall market and volatility backdrop safe to be trading in at all
Favorable — calm VIX, market in an uptrend
Neutral — elevated VIX, trend still intact
Unfavorable — high VIX, or market below trend
Then the rulebook decides, top to bottom
01
Chart is Weak→AVOID, full stop — nothing else can save it
02
Regime is Unfavorable→ capped at HOLD — a bad market overrides everything below
03
Financials Weak with nothing else strong→AVOID
04
Trend isn't real (ADX < 20), even if 2+ categories are Strong→HOLD, wait for it to confirm
05
Chart and Financials both Strong→BUY
06
2+ categories Strong, and Regime is Favorable or Neutral→BUY
07
Anything else→HOLD — mixed signals, wait for a cleaner setup
BUY HOLD AVOID a real three-way call, not all-or-nothing

Let's trace one input through both: Regime = Uptrend

VIX calm · S&P above its 200-day average

In the Simple Checklist

Uptrend regime lights up only question #8 ("is the whole market healthy?"). That's 1 of 9 bulbs.

The other eight — the stock's own trend, its momentum, its entry quality, its volume — still have to pass entirely on their own merits. A calm, healthy market doesn't rescue a stock that's flat, thin, or overextended.

Best case, a good regime gets you to 9/9 alongside eight other real passes — it's never enough by itself.

In the Full Analysis

Uptrend regime rates Risk/Macro as Favorable (or at least not Unfavorable). That removes Rule 02's ceiling — the verdict is no longer capped at HOLD no matter what.

But removing a ceiling isn't the same as reaching BUY. The stock still needs its own Technical and Fundamental picture to clear Rule 05 or Rule 06 — a healthy market with a Weak chart still lands on AVOID at Rule 01.

Flip it: Regime = Downtrend hits Rule 02 immediately — HOLD is the ceiling, no matter how good the stock looks underneath.
A good regime clears the way. It doesn't pick the stock.
Regime can only ever remove a ceiling (Full Analysis) or fill one bulb out of nine (Simple Checklist) — in both paths, the stock still has to earn its own case.

Q — 2026-07-28: "A stock I love just dropped 5% today, and the overall market is in a downtrend. Will either path still say buy it because it'll bounce back?"

Simple Checklist

No. Question 8 ("is the whole market healthy?") tests the S&P against its 200-day average — plain yes/no. If the market is genuinely in a downtrend, that one bulb fails, and this path has no override. It never asks "but is the stock itself great" — one failed bulb is enough to land on PASS, however good the other eight look.

A sudden 5% drop can also cost bulbs #1 (Trend), #5 (52-Wk Range), or #3 (Setup) if it breaks a key level — the real outcome is often worse than "8/9 minus regime," not just capped by it.

Full Analysis

Also no — but for a sharper reason. Rule 02 caps the verdict at HOLD whenever Risk/Macro is Unfavorable, and that rule runs before Technical and Fundamental are even weighed against each other. A textbook Strong+Strong stock still can't reach Rule 05 or 06 while Rule 02 has already capped it.

One real exception: if the market is shaky rather than broken (VIX elevated but S&P still above its 200-day line), Risk/Macro rates Neutral, not Unfavorable — and Neutral caps nothing. That's the one case where a great stock can still reach BUY through a rough day.

Neither path does "buy the dip because it'll bounce back."
That reasoning — a sharp drop making a stock more attractive — lives in a completely separate, automated-only engine (Mean-Reversion), not here. This tool's own Technical read says the opposite: RSI below 30 is flagged "deeply oversold — avoid catching falling knives." A sudden drop reads as a warning to Simple/Full Analysis, never as a discount.
Q — 2026-07-28: "How do the scanners help me pick the right stocks to analyze — and can one watch a group of stocks (or my own list) and tell me when something's about to break out?"

Getting a candidate list

Yes — that's the scanners' whole job (see the section above). "Best Candidates" is the sharpest one to reach for: it's the exact same query the automated Path A engine uses, so scanning it and analyzing what comes back is the closest thing to manually replaying what Path A already does, plus your own sector/regime read on top.

Watching a group for breakouts

Partly already built. The /breakout-watch skill and the Scan tab's badge column both work against any ticker list — your own included, not just Nirmal's default 20. Run it and get back who's confirmed, retesting, or on watch, right now.

What's missing is the "while I'm not looking" part — nothing runs on a schedule and alerts you unprompted. That's real, undone work (a scheduled watcher, same shape as the paper-trading engine's launchd job), not a setting to flip.
