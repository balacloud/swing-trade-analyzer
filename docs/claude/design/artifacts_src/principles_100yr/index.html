01Trend Following
Matches

Checks a stock against Minervini's 8-point "Trend Template": price above a rising 50-day, 150-day and 200-day average, at least 30% above its 52-week low, and within 25% of its 52-week high. Needs 7 of 8 to count as a real uptrend.

pattern_detection.py

The oldest rule in the book: only trade in the direction of the primary trend, and accept getting in a little late rather than trying to predict a turn. The simplest version is just "price above its 200-day average."

Dow Theory · Faber (2007)
02Momentum & Relative Strength
Partial

Requires a stock to simply be beating the S&P 500 over the past year (a loose "better than the market" bar) before it's considered strong — gated, one of 9 pass/fail checklist criteria. Update (Day 117): the stock's own absolute 1-year return is now shown right alongside it too, on both the Simple Checklist's Momentum card and the Full Analysis view — but purely as information, never gating anything.

simplifiedScoring.js · absoluteMomentum.js (Day 117)

Two ideas, not one: buy stocks already leading their peers (top ~20%, not just "beating the market"), and only if that stock is also up in absolute terms over the past year — otherwise sit in cash, as one combined, enforced rule. STA still doesn't combine them that way — that's still the real gap. Update (Day 111): the missing half was actually backtested as a gate (an absolute-momentum filter vs. a 5% risk-free proxy) — it came back clean but excluded only ~1% of trades, because the relative-strength check already does most of that filtering as a side effect (whenever the S&P's own trailing year clears the cash bar, beating the S&P already implies beating cash too). Decided not to pursue it as a gate: correct, but not worth the added complexity. Update (Day 117): the second half is no longer invisible, at least — it's now a readable line on screen, sourced from that same backtested 5% figure rather than a guess. It just isn't combined with the first half into one rule the way this principle actually prescribes, which is why this row stays Partial rather than moving to Match.

Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) · Antonacci, "Dual Momentum" (2014)
03Mean Reversion / Buying Dips
Matches

A separate strategy inside STA: buy a short-term oversold dip (2-day RSI under 10) only while the stock is still above its 200-day average — never in a downtrend. Exits on a bounce or after 10 days.

mean_reversion.py

The exact same idea, same math (2-day RSI, usually under 5), same guardrail (only above the 200-day average). This is one of the most literally research-backed rules in the whole list. Update (2026-09-15): this is no longer just a code-match claim — it's the one strategy in this whole document that got taken all the way to a real, unattended forward test: 241 closed live paper trades, 70.5% win rate, profit factor 2.00 when scored at the entry-day close; re-run at the next open (the price you can actually get): 63.5% wins, profit factor 1.50 (Day 123 correction) (final, every position now closed; this row showed a mid-September snapshot of 217 trades / PF 2.46 before), survived every cluster/overfitting stress-test run against it. The paper-trading program was discontinued 2026-09-12 (STA is now used as an analysis tool, not an automated-execution path) — but that number stands as the program's final result, and it's the strongest evidence anywhere in this project that a "Matches" verdict actually holds up live, not just on paper.

Larry Connors (2009)
04Volume Confirms the Move
Partial

STA's "Volume" check only asks "is there enough trading activity to get in and out easily" — a liquidity test, not a confirmation test — and that part is unchanged. Update (Day 111–112): a magnitude + direction read shipped on the Trade Setup card — today's volume vs. its 50-day average, plus a directional lean combining price change, close-location-in-range, and OBV trend.

Update (Day 116): this row's own principle — heavy volume with little price progress is a warning, not a green light — now has a direct read: today's volume crossed against today's price move, in units of the stock's own ATR. The existing 20-day OBV divergence sentence (previously buried in a hover tooltip nobody saw) is now visible text on the same card. Two real bugs were found and fixed along the way: OBV's trend label was silently biased toward "rising" for negative cumulative OBV (~3% of names affected); and the volume number itself was being read from the still-forming intraday bar, understating true participation for most of every trading day, not just this one card. All of it stays informational — a hard 1.5x-volume gate was tested first (Day 107) and cut trades 93%, too strict to keep, and "volume informs, never gates" is now a standing rule for this project. "Doesn't gate the decision" is still true; the read itself is now materially more complete and more honest than it was even a week ago.

simplifiedScoring.js · pattern_detection.py · backend.py · volumeThresholds.js (Day 111, 116)

One of the oldest technical ideas there is: a real breakout should come with a real jump in volume (roughly 40-50% above normal) — that's proof real money is behind the move. Heavy volume with little price progress is often a warning sign, not a green light.

Wyckoff (1910s-30s) · O'Neil / CANSLIM
05Position Sizing & Risk
Matches

Risks a fixed 2% of the account per trade by default, capped at 25% of the account in any one position, and automatically cuts position size in half when the VIX (market fear gauge) spikes above 30.

positionSizing.js · trade_simulator.py

Decide your maximum acceptable loss first, then size the trade backward from that — never size off "how confident you feel." The standard range across every source is roughly 1-2% of the account per trade, scaled by volatility.

Turtle Traders (1983) · Van Tharp
06Stop-Loss Placement
Matches

Stops are set from real volatility (ATR), not a flat guess — and once a trade is up enough, the stop only ever moves up to lock in gains, never back down. A stop that gaps through overnight fills honestly at the worse price, not the "ideal" one.

trade_simulator.py

Set the stop outside normal day-to-day noise using the stock's own volatility (2× its average true range is the classic figure), and once it's set, only ever tighten it — never widen a losing stop hoping it comes back.

Livermore (1940) · Turtle Traders
07Market Regime / Volatility Filter
Matches

Checks the VIX and whether the S&P is in an uptrend before giving any stock a green light — calm market + uptrend = favorable, VIX over 30 or a broken market trend caps every verdict at HOLD, no exceptions.

categoricalAssessment.js

Scale back exposure when the market gets more volatile — this is a risk-control rule, not a prediction that prices must fall. The original academic result scaled by recent realized volatility; scaling directly off the VIX (what STA does) is a widely-used real-world version of the same idea, not identical to the original study but a legitimate practical relative.

Moreira & Muir (2017)
08Sentiment as a Contrarian Signal
Matches

Reads the same CNN Fear & Greed Index this principle is built on, treats extreme readings with caution — and, correctly, never lets sentiment decide anything on its own; it's one of four inputs a stock has to pass. (One real bug found while checking this: the on-screen sentiment gauge's colors were quietly using outdated boundaries, different from the ones actually driving the decision — flagged below. Fixed Day 108, realigned to the correct 55/50/40 bands.)

categoricalAssessment.js

When fear or greed hits a real extreme, the crowd is usually positioned wrong — but treat it as a tilt, not a trigger. Extremes can persist for weeks, so acting on sentiment alone, without the price actually confirming a turn, is the classic way this gets misused.

Graham, "Mr. Market" (1949) · Neill (1954)
09Sector Rotation
Matches

Tracks which sectors are leading, weakening, lagging or improving relative to the market (a rotation map), and correctly treats it as background context, not a hard trading rule.

backend.py

Own the sectors currently showing the strongest relative performance, and rotate out as their ranking fades. (A separate, older tradition rotates by calendar season instead — STA doesn't do that version, which is fine; they're two different valid takes on the same underlying idea.)

de Kempenaer (RRG) · Moskowitz & Grinblatt

What the comparison shows

  1. Reddit looks at one trade at a time; the canon also looks at the whole market. Everything Reddit misses is about the wider market: whether a stock leads its peers, the market's mood, and which sectors lead.
  2. Reddit says what to do; the canon says how. "Have a stop" versus "about 2× ATR away." "Manage risk" versus "1–2% of the account." Reddit's version is easier to follow and harder to check.
  3. The one real conflict is RSI. Reddit is right about low RSI on its own. The canon's version, very low 2-day RSI but only above the 200-day average, is the one with evidence behind it.
  4. Reddit is stronger on behaviour. Patience, one setup and keeping records aren't in the canon, but they're how a trader actually follows rules. The two complement each other more than they compete.

Reddit is a good habit checklist for placing one trade. The canon is the evidence for where an edge comes from. Reddit on its own gives discipline but misses most of the market-wide filters.

Where Reddit and the canon disagree

Reddit says not to buy just because RSI is low. Row 03 says the opposite: buy when 2-day RSI is very low. Both are right about different versions.

Low RSI on its own, in any stock, is catching a falling knife. Low RSI on a liquid stock that is still above its 200-day average is Connors' rule, and it's STA's one strategy that held up live (241 trades, profit factor 1.50 when bought at the next open).

Known Inconsistencies Found While Auditing STA's Own Code

⚠Three different "good enough" risk/reward numbers coexist in the app at once — the main checklist wants 2:1, the automated trading gate (discontinued Sep 12) wanted 1.2:1, and an on-screen badge wants 1:1. Not a bug exactly, but there's no single honest answer to "what R:R does STA actually require."
⚠A code comment says one thing, the code does another. The mean-reversion engine's own documentation claims it "only trades when ADX is under 20" (a range-bound market) — the actual code never checks ADX at all when deciding to trade.
✓The Fear & Greed gauge you saw wasn't using the same math as the decision engine — FIXED Day 108. Found while checking this exact page — the real verdict logic used one set of boundaries; the backend had a second, older, unused set; the actual color bar on the Analyze page used a third, also outdated, set. All three realigned to the correct 55/50/40 bands. Kept here as a record of what auditing this document itself turned up, not because it's still open.
