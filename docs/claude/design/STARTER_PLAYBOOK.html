Tab 1 · Start here

What kind of trader are you?

Before any rules, pick a style that fits your job, your nerves and the time you actually have. Scalper, day, swing and position trading differ in how long you hold a trade. Directional trading describes something else: what you're betting on. It can apply to any of the four.

Quick self-check

  1. Can you watch prices during market hours? If not, day trading and scalping are out. That leaves swing or position.
  2. Can you sleep with an open trade? Swing and position trades are held overnight and over weekends, and prices can jump at the open.
  3. How long can you wait for a result? If you need to know within days, swing. If you're fine waiting months, position.

Every trade answers four questions

  1. Which way is it going? Only trade in the direction of the trend.
  2. Is this a good moment? Wait for a dip or a quiet pause. Don't chase a big run.
  3. Where am I wrong? Know the price that proves the idea failed, before you buy.
  4. How much? Size the trade so being wrong is a small, survivable loss.

Tab 3 · What traders on Reddit say

The Reddit checklist: 10 rules

These are the swing-trading rules that come up again and again in beginner threads on Reddit. Five of them are entry rules you check on a chart. The other five are habits. Purple tags show where a rule matches the 100-year canon in tab 4.

The five entry rules · check on a daily chart before every trade

1

Trade with the trend Canon 01

Price vs its 200-day moving average.

Passes when the last close is above the 200-day average.

2

Wait for a pullback Canon 03

RSI(2), or the distance to the 21-day average.

Passes when RSI(2) is below 10, or price has dipped to the 21-day average, and rule 1 passes.

3

Know support and resistance

Recent swing lows (support) and swing highs (resistance).

Passes when there is support close below the price, so your stop has a sensible place to go.

4

Watch consolidation part of Canon 04

The high-to-low range of the last 20 days.

Passes when the range has narrowed and gone quiet. A break above it is the signal.

5

Keep indicators simple

Only the six indicators below.

Passes when you can explain the trade in one sentence without opening another indicator.

You decide how many rules must pass. Write that number down and stick to it. These thresholds are common starting points, not numbers proven for every market.

Tab 4 · A hundred years of trading

The 100-year canon: 9 principles

These ideas have held up in books and studies from the 1900s to now. Reddit rediscovers most of them, and orange tags show where. Two of them (sentiment and sector rotation) almost never come up on Reddit.

01

Trend following

Dow Theory · Faber (2007)

Prices that are rising tend to keep rising. Trade in the direction of the trend.

Beginner use: only buy above the 200-day average.

Misuse: it is late by design, so expect to miss tops and bottoms.

Reddit rule 1
02

Momentum and relative strength

Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) · Antonacci, Dual Momentum (2014)

Winners over the last 3–12 months tend to keep beating losers, so favour leaders over laggards.

Beginner use: compare your stock with the market (SPY) and its peers.

Misuse: momentum crashes hard when the market turns.

03

Mean reversion

Larry Connors (2009)

Short, sharp dips inside an uptrend tend to bounce back.

Beginner use: RSI(2) below 10 while above the 200-day.

Misuse: buying dips in a downtrend means catching falling knives.

Reddit rule 2
04

Volume confirms the move

Wyckoff (1910s–30s) · O'Neil, CANSLIM

Real moves come with real volume. A breakout from a base should come on heavy trading.

Beginner use: look for relative volume of 1.5× or more on a breakout day.

Misuse: news spikes can look like accumulation.

Reddit rule 4 (part)
05

Position sizing

Turtle Traders (1983) · Van Tharp

How much you buy matters more than what you buy. Size every trade from the risk, not from a hunch.

Beginner use: pick your stop first, then work out the share count.

Misuse: doubling up after a loss to "win it back".

Reddit habit: risk first
06

Stop placement

Livermore (1940) · Turtle Traders

Decide where you're wrong before you enter, and set the stop from real volatility (ATR), not a round number.

Beginner use: place it below support and beyond about 1–2 ATR.

Misuse: moving the stop further away when price gets close.

Reddit habit: entry, stop, target
07

Market regime

Dow Theory · Faber (2007) · Moreira & Muir (2017)

Most stocks follow the market. When the market is in a downtrend or very volatile, good setups fail more often.

Beginner use: check SPY vs its 200-day and the VIX before you trade.

Misuse: ignoring it because your one stock looks great.

08

Sentiment as a contrarian signal

CNN Fear & Greed Index · long-standing market lore

When fear or greed hits an extreme, the crowd is usually on the wrong side.

Beginner use: treat it as a tilt, not a trigger.

Misuse: extremes can last for weeks. Wait for price to confirm the turn.

09

Sector rotation

Business-cycle investing · sector rotation maps

Money moves between sectors in waves. Stocks in a leading sector have the wind at their backs.

Beginner use: check whether the stock's sector ETF is above its own 200-day.

Misuse: chasing last month's hot sector after the move is done.

Tab 5 · If you build it in code

Golden rules for coding a trading system

These ten come from building and running a real swing-trading system with Claude Code: over 120 working sessions and dozens of bugs caught. Each one is here because breaking it once cost real time or produced a false result. They apply whether you code it yourself or have an AI write it.

1

Never trust a result nobody ran

"The backtest shows 60% wins" means nothing until you've seen it run and read its output. Ask the AI for the command and the raw numbers.

2

Check every item, not a sample

A spot-check once passed 93% of items. Checking all of them found only 21% were actually correct.

3

Freeze the rules before you forward-test

Every time you peek at the test period and tweak a number, that period stops being a test. Tweaking until the past looks good is fooling yourself.

4

Use equal weights; don't optimize on a small sample

With a few hundred trades, "tuned" weights fit noise. Simple equal weights usually hold up better out of sample.

5

Backtest and live must share one piece of code

Two copies of the same rule drift apart. Test that they match across many inputs, not just a few hand-picked ones.

6

Drop today's unfinished bar

During market hours, data providers include today's half-formed candle. It is not a close. Only count a bar after the market shuts.

7

Date records from the data, not the computer's clock

A job run on a weekend or holiday stamps the wrong day and creates signals that never happened.

8

A price jump between data sources may be a split

Before calling it a bug, check for a stock split. A 3-for-1 split looks like a 66% crash in unadjusted data.

9

Lock the stop and target in when you enter

Recalculating them every day lets the plan quietly move under an open trade. Save them once, then only read them back.

10

Review every fix three times, each pass harder

Pass 1: does it work? Pass 2: assume it missed an edge case. Pass 3: what breaks it in real use? Most bugs hide in pass 2 and 3.

Tab 6 · General

The anatomy of a market move

Big moves tend to unfold in four stages. Knowing which stage you're in tells you whether you're early, on time or late.

  1. 1

    The catalyst

    A major fundamental spark, such as an unexpected central bank rate cut or a blowout earnings report, instantly shifts market expectations.

    On the chart: a gap or one very large candle.

  2. 2

    Institutional flow

    The real heavyweight move comes when hedge funds, mutual funds and pension funds move billions of dollars based on the new information.

    On the chart: heavy volume for days and a steady trend.

  3. 3

    Retail momentum

    Smaller investors notice the new trend and jump in, making the move faster in whichever direction it is going.

    On the chart: a steep move with RSI stretched above 70.

  4. 4

    Valuation re-anchoring

    The frenzy cools, and prices settle at a realistic multiple of the company's actual earnings.

    On the chart: a sideways range, which is consolidation.

The core logic · about 30 lines of Python

import pandas as pd

def add_indicators(df):
    c = df["Close"]
    df["sma200"] = c.rolling(200).mean()
    df["ema21"]  = c.ewm(span=21, adjust=False).mean()
    d  = c.diff()
    up = d.clip(lower=0).ewm(alpha=1/2, adjust=False).mean()
    dn = (-d.clip(upper=0)).ewm(alpha=1/2, adjust=False).mean()
    df["rsi2"]  = 100 - 100 / (1 + up / dn)
    tr = pd.concat([df["High"] - df["Low"],
                    (df["High"] - c.shift()).abs(),
                    (df["Low"]  - c.shift()).abs()], axis=1).max(axis=1)
    df["atr14"] = tr.rolling(14).mean()
    df["rvol"]  = df["Volume"] / df["Volume"].rolling(50).mean()
    return df

def check_rules(df):
    x = df.iloc[-1]
    support = df["Low"].iloc[-20:].min()
    r1 = x.Close > x.sma200
    r2 = r1 and (x.rsi2 < 10 or abs(x.Close - x.ema21) <= x.atr14)
    r3 = 0 <= x.Close - support <= 1.5 * x.atr14
    stop   = min(support - 0.25 * x.atr14, x.Close - 1.5 * x.atr14)
    target = df["High"].iloc[-60:].max()
    return {"rules": [r1, r2, r3], "score": sum([r1, r2, r3]),
            "entry": x.Close, "stop": stop, "target": target,
            "reward_risk": (target - x.Close) / (x.Close - stop)}

Only use bars that have closed. If you run this during market hours, drop today's unfinished bar first (coding rule 6).

Where TradingView's chart library goes

Lightweight Charts is TradingView's free, open-source chart library. It only draws charts and does no maths, so it belongs in the frontend, on the Ticker detail screen. The backend sends the bars and the stop and target. The chart just displays them.

  1. Install it inside the frontend folder (shown on the right).
  2. Use it in TickerDetail.jsx.
  3. Or simply tell Claude Code: "Add a TradingView Lightweight Charts candlestick chart to TickerDetail.jsx, with the 200-day SMA as a line and the stop and target as horizontal price lines from /api/ticker/{symbol}."
